INTENTIONS

Learning Competency and Curriculum Standards

Realize the value of doing philosophy in obtaining a broad perspective on life (PPT11/12-Ib-1.2).

Learning Objectives

Knowledge: Differentiate between a ground-level partial perspective and a holistic "helicopter" perspective when evaluating life experiences.

Skills: Execute a systematic "Philosophical Audit" on a personal, everyday routine using Socratic questioning to extract a broader life perspective.

Behavior: Value open-mindedness by deliberately pausing automated behaviors to reflect on personal choices and long-term consequences.

Pre-Lesson

Directions: The Zoom-Out Mystery Game!

The "End of the World" Free-Write (Concrete Experience)

 

Students will do a private, silent 3-minute free-write. They must recall a past event in their lives that felt absolutely devastating or like the "end of the world" at the time (e.g., failing a grade, a painful breakup, losing a major competition), focusing heavily on how they felt in that exact moment.

 

Scoring mechanism:

  • 2 Points: Completed the reflection with explicit focus on their past raw emotions.
  • 1 Point: Completed the reflection superficially without emotional depth.

DAY 1

 

·         CORE INQUIRY

Realize the value of doing philosophy in obtaining a broad perspective on life (PPT11/12-Ib-1.2).

·         GROUNDING THE MIND

Directions: On your fingers, show the class your stress levels regarding your current biggest worry, where 5 is "completely overwhelmed" and 1 is "totally calm”.

 

·         PHILOSOPHICAL INSIGHT

The teacher introduces The Helicopter View framework to process the opening activity:

 

The Ground-Level View (Partial Perspective): The teacher points out that during their most devastating past event, the students had a narrow focus, blinded by immediate emotions and necessity.

 

The Helicopter View (Holistic Perspective): The teacher will guide students to realize how flying 1,000 feet above that memory allows them to see that event actually shaped them for the better. The teacher will provide sample realization like "Failure is not a permanent identity; it is simply a course correction."

 

COLLECTIVE EXAMINATION (Guided Reflection)

The teacher divides the board into "Partial POV" and "Holistic Perspective". To prepare students for their practical performance task, the class collectively analyzes a common, automatic teenage routine: Checking social media notifications immediately upon waking up.

·         The class tracks the Partial View: Instant dopamine, fear of missing out (FOMO).

·         The class builds the Holistic View: How this single 10-minute automated choice frames the brain's anxiety levels, time management, and attention span for the rest of the day.

 

DAY 2

INDEPENDENT PRAXIS

Directions: Students launch their major performance task: The Philosophical Audit.

·         Now that we have finished examining the past, it is time to face the present. Students will identify a current problem or struggle and express through writing what they think and feel about it, as well as how they are responding to that struggle.

·         They must subject this concrete problem to a rigorous Socratic Interrogation by mapping out answers to these three criteria in sheet of paper:

1.    The Hidden Value: What minor or temporary value am I prioritizing while i am facing this challenge?

2.    The Blindspot: What broad contexts, consequences, or alternative choices am I completely ignoring?

3.    The Horizon: If I view this problem from a "helicopter perspective," how is it quietly shaping the person I will become in five years?

 

DAY 3: THE AGENCY PIVOT (PART 1)

STOIC MATRIX INTERVENTION (Categorizing Control)

·         Directions: The teacher introduces Epictetus' classic Stoic concept: The Dichotomy of Control.

o    The Philosophy: A primary source of human suffering (partial perspective) is worrying about things we cannot control, while neglecting the things we can control.

·         Task: Students divide a blank page into two columns to analyze their presented problem during day 2:

o    Column A: Outside My Control (The Externals): (e.g., other people’s opinions, the difficulty of an upcoming exam, past mistakes, traffic, weather).

o    Column B: Within My Control (My Agency): (e.g., my preparation, how I speak to myself, when I study, my willingness to seek help, my breathing).

 

DAY 4: THE AGENCY PIVOT (PART 2)

DIALECTICAL COUNSELING (Peer Collaboration)

·         Directions: In pairs, students take turns playing the role of a "Philosophical Counselor."

·         The Process:

o    Student A shares their Column B (Within My Control) list regarding their struggle.

o    Student B (The Counselor) listens and asks: "Is there anything on this list that is secretly out of your control? And is there any hidden power you forgot to list?"

o    They switch roles. This ensures they are focusing strictly on actionable agencies, broadening their perspective on what they can solve.

 

LET’S REFLECT ON IT!

·         Directions: On a 1/4 sheet of paper, have students write down their personal plan and hand it in before leaving:

It should look like:

 

·         "I used to feel completely overwhelmed by [My Struggle] because I was trying to control [What I Cannot Control]. By taking a step back, I see that my real power is in [What I Can Control]. Today, I chose to focus on this by [One Specific Action I Will Take Today]."

Opportunities for integration

This lesson directly integrates with Personal Development (PerDev) (emotional regulation, proactive problem-solving) and Media and Information Literacy (MIL) (distinguishing between uncontrolled algorithmic stimuli and our active, cognitive responses to them).

 

ASSESSMENT

Formative Assessment

Check the submitted sheet of paper “The Philosophical Audit”. Look for an authentic shift in perspective: Did the student successfully move from feeling stuck and helpless (focusing on things outside their control) to finding a practical, positive step they can take (focusing on their agency)?

 

Note: A rubric is attached on a separate page to assess this activity.

WAYS FORWARD

Extended learning opportunities

BROADENING PERSPECTIVE:

 

  • Support (Remediation): Students with vague goals get a target card to focus on just one minor, 5-minute actionable daily task (e.g., setting out early to attend class every day without fail).

 

  • Extension (The Community Helicopter): Students analyze a local community issue (e.g., flooding or waste management), identifying where people waste energy on uncontrollable factors versus one practical youth-led initiative they can execute.
